Marketplace Fulfillment Quality
Independent case · simulated data
Operations analytics case study

Fulfillment quality shouldn’t fail silently.

I analyzed 100,000 simulated marketplace orders to detect a sustained cancellation-rate deterioration, size its impact, identify which failure modes grew, isolate store concentration, and test whether common monitoring rules would catch it.

A deterioration is intentionally injected into selected Retailer C stores from September 1, 2026. No real company data is used.

07 · Operational follow-up

Investigate where the evidence is concentrated.

The findings support targeted investigation, not a claim that the descriptive drivers caused a real-world event.

01

Audit inventory synchronization

Start with flagged evening stores and retailer-initiated out-of-stock events.

02

Validate store-hour accuracy

Twenty of 29 early-closure cancellations occur in evening windows.

03

Pair adaptive and fixed monitoring

Do not rely on a rolling threshold alone for sustained shifts.

04

Measure intervention impact

Re-run cancellation, fill-rate, reschedule and timeliness metrics after corrective action.

Methodology & limitations

Simulated by design

All data is simulated and the Retailer C September deterioration is intentionally injected. No proprietary marketplace data is used.

Uncertainty

Rate charts show 95% Wilson intervals. Two-proportion z-tests are reported for the main rate comparisons. Small cells remain less stable.

Store flags

Expected store cancellations use Retailer C Jul–Aug daypart rates. Poisson z ≥ 3 is an illustrative prioritization rule. Ranking is ex-post.

Monitoring

Thresholds are illustrative and were not optimized against a real false-positive or intervention-cost function. Alert days and episodes are reported separately.
